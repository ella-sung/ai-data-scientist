What the analysis found

Plan tier is the strongest structural split

Churn falls monotonically as plan value rises. Starter accounts churn at 23.7%, roughly 24x the enterprise rate. This is partly a contract effect (most starter accounts are month-to-month) and partly an engagement effect, since starter accounts log in less and use fewer features.

starter 23.7%, pro 7.4%, business 4.3%, enterprise 1.0%

Dormancy is the earliest reliable warning

Churn climbs steeply with days since last login. Accounts quiet for more than 60 days churn at 66.7%, against 9.1% for accounts active within the week. Because login recency is observable daily, it is the practical trigger for intervention rather than a post-hoc explanation.

0-7 days: 9.1%; 8-14 days: 16.1%; 15-30 days: 22.7%; 31-60 days: 47.4%; 60+ days: 66.7%

The model relies on behaviour, not demographics

Permutation importance on the held-out set is led by breadth of feature use, NPS and account size, with login recency and support volume close behind. Region and industry contribute almost nothing. That is encouraging: the drivers are things the business can act on, not fixed attributes of who the customer is.

Read this before acting

  • exit_survey_sent was dropped before modelling. It is only ever set after an account cancels, so including it would have produced an AUC near 0.96 that meant nothing.
  • 140 duplicate rows were removed before splitting. Left in, they leak across the train/test boundary and inflate every metric.
  • This is a cross-sectional snapshot, not a time-based split, so the numbers describe association rather than forecast performance on future cohorts.
  • NPS is missing for 11% of accounts and missingness is not random; low-engagement accounts answer surveys less often.

Recommended actions

  1. Trigger a customer-success touch at 14 days of inactivity on any account above $100 MRR, rather than waiting for the renewal date.
  2. Route payment failures on starter and pro plans to a retry-plus-outreach flow; they carry the largest single-variable effect after dormancy.
  3. Offer annual terms at a discount to month-to-month starter accounts, since contract length shows an independent protective effect.
  4. Score the book weekly and work the top decile, which contains 70% of eventual churners.
