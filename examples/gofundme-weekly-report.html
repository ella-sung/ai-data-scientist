    What the analysis found

    Take rate is falling faster than volume is growing

    Take rate has gone from 3.48% to 3.01% of GDV, driven almost entirely by voluntary donor tips falling from 2.96% to 2.49%. Processing margin is stable, so this is a checkout behaviour problem, not a cost problem. The decline is monotonic week over week rather than seasonal, which is what separates it from the volume line: GDV over this window is confounded by the summer giving trough, but the tip rate falls steadily through it. At current volume the compression costs about $385K per week on donations the platform is already processing. None of this is visible on a volume chart, which is the argument for putting take rate on the dashboard as a first-class metric rather than deriving it on request.

    tips 2.96% to 2.49% of GDV; net revenue -14.1% over 27 weeks

    A first-day donation is the strongest liquidity signal

    Only 12.5% of campaigns reach 100% of goal, but campaigns collectively raise 35% of the goals they set, so partial funding is the norm rather than failure and the fully-funded rate on its own is a misleading health measure. Across categories the share of campaigns receiving a donation within 24 hours correlates 0.85 with eventual goal attainment. Memorial campaigns start fastest and attain most; education campaigns start slowest and attain least. That makes the first day the highest-leverage moment on the platform.

    60.4% of campaigns funded within 24h; attainment 35%

    Blended CAC hides what paid acquisition actually costs

    Only 48% of campaigns come from paid channels. Blended cost per campaign is $24.25, but the real cost of a bought campaign is $50.83 — roughly 2x higher. Reporting the blended figure would make paid acquisition look far cheaper than it is. Meanwhile 27% of new campaigns are started by people who first arrived as donors, which is the loop actually driving growth.

    paid CAC $50.83 vs blended $24.25 at 48% paid share

    Fraud is concentrating in emergency relief and getting worse

    The share of emergency-relief campaigns flagged for fraud has risen from 0.310% to 0.419%, against 0.102% across every other category. Disaster-driven categories are spiky and time-pressured, which is exactly the environment bad actors exploit. Its refund rate is also the platform's highest at 1.63%. For a donation platform this is not a cost line, it is the product: donor trust is the asset being spent.

    Medical fraud 0.105%, refunds 0.62%; Memorial fraud 0.100%, refunds 0.62%; Nonprofit fraud 0.106%, refunds 0.62%

    The subscription line is growing through expansion, not new logos

    Net revenue retention on the pre-window cohort is 89% against gross retention of 83% and logo retention of 77%. The gap between NRR and GRR is expansion within surviving accounts, which is carrying the line while account count shrinks. That is a healthier position than the logo number alone suggests, but it concentrates revenue in fewer customers.

    Read this before acting

    • Synthetic data. GoFundMe does not publish these figures; the structure reflects their public business model but every number here is invented.
    • Take rate here counts donor tips plus processing revenue net of network cost. It excludes chargeback losses, fraud write-offs and support cost, so it is a gross take rate rather than a contribution margin.
    • The 24-hour-start to attainment correlation is measured across seven category aggregates, not across campaigns. Seven points is far too few to treat as causal, and an early donation is plausibly a proxy for the organizer having a mobilised network rather than a cause of funding. Prompting a share will not manufacture the network.
    • Week 0 falls in February and week 26 in August, so any first-to-last comparison of volume confounds trend with the summer giving trough. The tip-rate decline is stated instead because it is monotonic within the window and does not depend on that comparison.
    • Channel attribution is last-touch, so organic almost certainly absorbs credit for demand paid channels created upstream. The gap between paid and blended CAC is real but its size is attribution-dependent.
    • Fraud-flagged is a detection rate, not a fraud rate. A rise can mean more fraud or better detection, and these data cannot distinguish them.

    Recommended actions

    1. Treat tip rate as an owned metric with a named owner and a weekly target. A/B the tip prompt at checkout before adding volume, since a 0.1pp recovery is worth more than a week of GDV growth at current margins.
    2. Build a first-24-hour intervention for campaigns with no donations: prompt the organizer to share, since that window predicts the outcome better than anything measurable later.
    3. Report paid CAC and paid share separately from blended CAC in this review, and stop quoting the blended figure in acquisition decisions.
    4. Escalate emergency-relief verification: manual review above a volume threshold during disaster surges, when both the fraud rate and payout pressure peak simultaneously.
    5. Add chargeback losses and fraud write-offs to the take-rate calculation so the reported margin reflects money actually kept.
